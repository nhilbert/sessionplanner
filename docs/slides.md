# Slides – format, catalogue and editor (draft 2, 2026-11-01)

Slides for the agenda and the library. A slide is text in a small HTML subset; one stylesheet owned by the app gives every slide its look, and a theme (colours, fonts, logo) is chosen per workshop. Decks are presented from the app and exported as an editable PowerPoint file, as a PDF or as one standalone HTML file. Slides are edited in the app as content (section 7): fields for the words, a catalogue layout chosen per slide, the appearance from the theme; themes and template slides are edited there too. Existing PowerPoint decks are translated once (section 6); the way back is an export, not a round trip.

Stages 1 to 3 (format and catalogue, translation workflow, slides in the app) are done; stage 4 (this draft) adds the editor, themes as data and template slides. How the layouts should look – grid, type scale, compositions, signature devices – is decided in [slide-design.md](slide-design.md).

## 1. Decisions

- **Content, not pictures.** A slide holds structure and text only. Appearance lives in `slides.css`, which is why every deck looks the same, light and dark both work, previews are crisp at any size, text is searchable, and an agent can read and write slides.
- **One design size**, 1280 × 720 px. The host scales a slide to the thumbnail, the item panel, the full screen or the printed page; nothing in the slide knows about its size.
- **A fixed catalogue of layouts** (section 3) instead of free positioning. Every slide picks one layout; what the layout does not offer is not possible. The catalogue was derived from eight real decks and covers what recurs in them.
- **Themes are data.** The repository ships a neutral default theme. The owner's themes are kept as data in the database – ten colours, two fonts, a few choices and the logo – and edited in the app (section 4); the stylesheet is generated from them. Theme folders in the data folder (a hand-written stylesheet with its own fonts) still work, for a partner's brand that needs more than the tokens offer. A theme is chosen per workshop.
- **Content is edited as fields, not as pixels.** The editor (section 7) shows the slide's words in fields next to the live slide, the layout is picked from the catalogue and carries the content along, and nothing can be dragged or resized: what the layout does not offer is not possible, which is what keeps every deck looking the same. With a mouse the same fields are filled on the slide itself (2026-11-17): a click on a text types into it, a click on a picture replaces or removes it.
- **Formatting by hand names the theme's means, never values** (2026-11-17). A word or a line can be set larger or smaller, in a colour, bold, italic or not bold; the slide stores a size step and a place of the palette, so the formatting stays when the theme changes and the theme decides what it looks like (section 2).
- **Illustrations stay pictures.** Photos and ink-wash illustrations are assets referenced from the slide, stored once by content hash. Simple shapes, matrices and models are inline SVG so they stay sharp and follow the theme.
- **PowerPoint as an export, not a round trip** (2026-11-04). The deck is written as a PPTX of native text boxes, shapes and pictures, so it can be edited and handed on; it is not read back. What the format guarantees (layouts, themes, the editor's fields) ends at the export.

## 2. The slide

```html
<section class="slide cards">
  <header>
    <div class="eyebrow">Tag 1 · Führungskonzepte</div>
    <h2>Was ist Führung?</h2>
    <p class="lead">Ein bewusst schlanker Theorie-Input.</p>
  </header>
  <ul class="cards cols-2">
    <li><h3>Führung ist eine Rolle</h3><p>Keine Persönlichkeitseigenschaft …</p></li>
    <li><h3>Führung wirkt im System</h3><p>Geführt wird ein soziales System …</p></li>
  </ul>
  <footer><span class="source">Quelle: …</span></footer>
</section>
```

- `section.slide` carries the layout as a second class. Inside: an optional `header` (eyebrow, title `h2`, lead), one content block, an optional `footer` with the source. Title, section, statement, quote, break and closing slides have no header; their text sits in a `div.text`.
- The host adds what the slide must not contain: the footer line with deck name and number (themes that show one), the logo, and the scaling.
- **Allowed elements**: `section header footer div span h1 h2 h3 p ul ol li dl dt dd strong em br blockquote cite figure figcaption img table thead tbody tr th td aside code pre` and the SVG subset `svg g path rect circle ellipse line polyline polygon text tspan`.
- **Allowed attributes**: `class`, `alt`, `src` (assets only, see below), `viewBox`, `role`, `aria-label`, the SVG geometry and presentation attributes (`x y width height r rx cx cy d points x1 y1 x2 y2 transform fill fill-opacity stroke stroke-width stroke-linecap stroke-linejoin stroke-dasharray text-anchor font-size opacity`).
- **Forbidden**: `style`, `id`, event attributes, `script`, `iframe`, `foreignObject`, `use`, external addresses of any kind. Colours in SVG come from the classes in section 4, never from literal values. The server sanitises on write with this allowlist; the browser sanitises again before rendering.
- **Formatting by hand**: a `span` that carries only formatting classes, around a word or a line. Sizes `fs-xs fs-s fs-l fs-xl` are steps relative to the size the layout gives the text (0.72, 0.86, 1.2 and 1.45 em), so a larger word is larger in every theme and layout. Colours are places of the theme's palette: `accent` (the deeper accent, as before), `c-accent-2 c-accent-3 c-accent-4`, `c-title`, `c-text`, `muted`. `fw-normal` sets a word of a heading not bold; `strong` and `em` are bold and italic. One span may carry a size, a colour and `fw-normal` together. No literal colour and no pixel size can be written (`style` stays forbidden), which is what lets a slide change its theme and still read: a dark theme gives the same places its own colours. What the theme cannot promise is the contrast of a palette colour on the field colour of a section or closing slide; the theme editor's check does not cover it. The rules stand last in `slides.css`, so they hold against the layouts' own.
- **Assets**: `<img src="asset:<hash>">`. The host rewrites the reference to its API, which checks that the requester may see a workshop or library entry that uses the asset. Images are uploaded through the app or the import script; the slide text never carries image data.
- **Design size**: slides are written for 1280 × 720. Font sizes in the stylesheet are in pixels of that canvas; the host scales.

## 3. Layout catalogue

| Layout | Used for | Markup |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | first slide of a workshop or day | `div.text` with `.eyebrow`, `h1`, `p.subtitle`, `p.meta`; optional `figure`, a column at the right edge (a theme may fill the slide with it under a scrim) |
| `section` | a divider between topics | `div.text` with `.eyebrow`, `h1`, optional `.lead`, `ul.list`; optional `figure`, a band across the top; optional `.glyph` |
| `agenda` | the plan of a day, numbered, with optional times or a picture | `ol.agenda > li > span.when? + span`; or `ol.blocks` cards with time ranges; `.cols` when a figure sits next to the list |
| `break` | a pause with its end time | `div.text` with `h1`, `p.until`; optional `figure` |
| `closing` | the last slide | `img.logo`, `h1`, `p` on the theme's field colour |
| `statement` | one sentence that carries the slide, a question for the group | `div.text` with `.eyebrow?`, `p.big`, `p.sub?` |
| `quote` | a quotation with attribution | `blockquote > p + p.sub?`, `cite` |
| `list` | bullet points, one or two levels; rules; literature; points with a heading each | `ul.list` or `ol.list`, modifiers `.compact`, `.two`, `.rows` (a `strong` heading on each point) |
| `cards` | two to five parallel points with heading and text, optional picture, number or short list | `ul.cards.cols-N > li > img.icon? + .eyebrow? + h3 + (p | ul.list)`; modifiers `.numbered`, `.plain` (no surface, picture cropped to a band), `.plain.contain` (illustrations shown whole, labels aligned below), `.picture` (picture fills the top of the card), `.picture.contain` (book covers); cards fill the height they have |
| `split` | text beside a picture or diagram; a definition with an illustration | `div.split > div.text + figure`; the picture a column to the slide's edges, cropped; modifiers `.figure-left`, `.wide-text`, `.contain` (a cut-out or a diagram shown whole; a caption or an svg does the same) |
| `figure` | one large picture with caption | `figure.main > img + figcaption`; modifier `.full` |
| `exercise` | an exercise: the task, its steps, optionally the facts | `header` with `h2 > span.tag + title` and `p.lead` (the question or goal), then `div.exercise > ol.steps + aside.facts? > dl` |
| `steps` | a sequence or process, three to five steps | `ol.flow > li > .eyebrow? + h3 + p` |
| `matrix` | a 2 × 2 with axis labels | `div.matrix > .axis-y + .cells > .cell×4 + .axis-x`; `.cell.accent` |
| `acronym` | letters and their meaning (GROW, VUCA, SCAMPER) | `dl.acronym > div > dt + dd > strong + span`; modifier `.compact` |
| `canvas` | a grid of labelled boxes with guiding questions | `div.canvas.cols-N > .box > h3 + p`; `.box.wide`, `.box.tall`, `.box.accent` |
| `diagram` | two to five entries as a shape drawn from their number: a pyramid, a cycle (a ring with an open middle), overlapping circles, a core with rings, pillars under a roof, a hub | `div.diagram.SHAPE > ol > li > h3 + p?`, then `p.core?` (the word in the middle, in the overlap, on the roof); SHAPE is `pyramid`, `cycle`, `venn`, `onion`, `pillars` or `hub`; the drawing (`svg`) and the count (`n-N`) are put in by the app |
| `model` | a diagram as inline SVG: pyramid, cycle, curve, triangle | `figure.model > svg + figcaption?` |
| `table` | a small table | `table`, `td.good`, `td.bad`, modifier `.compact` |
| `number` | one large figure with its evidence, or two to four side by side | `ul.numbers > li > p.value + p.label` |
| `compare` | two columns against each other: before and after, option A and B, pro and contra | `div.compare > div.side × 2 > h3 + p? + ul.list?` |
| `timeline` | three to six dated points on one line | `ol.timeline > li > .eyebrow (the date) + h3 + p?` |

Title, agenda, break and closing slides are the ones the app can generate from the workshop (section 5 of the concept); the others are written. The facts panel of an exercise (duration, format, material) can be filled from the item.

**Diagrams** (`src/domain/diagram.ts`). A diagram slide holds its entries as a list; the shape is computed from how many there are, as inline SVG in the theme's classes with no words in it but the numerals. `normalizeSlideHtml` draws it, so the drawing is made wherever a slide is stored or shown, whoever wrote the markup, and can never differ from the entries: an agent writes the list only, the editor adds an entry and the pyramid has a layer more, the shape is changed by its class with the words kept. The words are text of the slide, laid over the drawing by `slides.css` from the same numbers (the box is 1152 × 470 and keeps that ratio): beside the layers of a pyramid and the rings of an onion, clockwise on both sides of a cycle, the circles and a hub, inside the pillars. Ordered shapes (pyramid, onion, cycle) are tints of the accent, the circles take the palette's accents. Two to five entries is the room; one and six are drawn too. The ring's class is `cycle` because `ring` is a utility class of the app's own stylesheet. The shapes were chosen by what recurs among the hand-drawn `model` slides of the real decks; charts, flowcharts and one-off drawings stay `model`.

Common elements: `.eyebrow` (kicker above the title), `.tag` (the kicker in front of an exercise title, later derived from the item's category), `.lead`, `.takeaway` (the one-line takeaway in the footer of a content slide), `.source`, `.muted`, `.accent`.

The facts panel of an exercise (`aside.facts`: duration, format, material, and the result the group brings back) is optional. It repeats on the wall what the facilitator knows from the plan, so participants see how long they have and what to use; in the app it can be filled from the item instead of being written into the slide.

## 4. Themes

A theme sets the tokens slides.css reads on `[data-theme="name"]` and may add decorations for the title, section and closing slides (a picture scrim, where the logo sits):

- colours: `--bg --surface --surface-2 --line --fg --fg-2 --fg-3 --title --accent --accent-deep --accent-2 --accent-3 --accent-4 --on-accent --field --on-field --on-field-2 --tint` (the accent mixed into the ground: the one panel the format fills) and optionally `--kicker` (small capitals and numerals; the deeper accent unless set)
- type: `--font-display --font-body --font-mono --display-weight` (800 unless set) `--numeral-weight` (300 unless set: the large thin numerals)
- marks: `--mark-size --mark-radius` (the small mark in the accent colour before an unnumbered point: 9 px square unless set; a theme as data chooses square, circle or none)
- shape and chrome: `--radius --chrome` (whether the host's footer line shows), `--logo --logo-light --mark`; the footer line carries the slide number, the deck name and, once the deck has two or more section slides, the section tracker ("Teil 2 von 5") from the first section on

SVG in slides uses classes bound to these tokens: fills `f-accent f-accent-deep f-accent-2 f-accent-3 f-accent-4 f-surface f-surface-2 f-title f-bg`, strokes `s-accent s-line s-fg`, text `t t-small t-display t-on`.

**Themes as data** (`src/domain/theme.ts`, table `themes`, Slides → Themes in the navigation). A theme the owner makes in the app holds:

- ten colours: background, text, title, four accents, text on accent, the field colour of section and closing slides and the text on it. The other tokens (surfaces, line, the two quieter text tones, the deeper accent, the quieter text on the field) are derived from these by mixing in OKLab, so they stay in step when a base colour changes; each can be overridden in the editor's advanced section;
- two fonts, display and body, from a short list the app hosts (Inter, Manrope, Lato, Source Serif 4) or the viewer's system stacks, and the heading weight (500 to 900);
- the corner radius, the footer line on or off, whether a picture on the title slide gets a dark gradient with light text, and where the logo shows (closing slide only, title slide, every slide);
- a logo and a light variant for the field colour, uploaded like the printout logo (PNG, JPEG, WebP or SVG, 2 MB), stored in `data/files/theme-logos`; the logo of a light theme also heads the printouts of every workshop in the theme (docs/concept.md, section 6; a dark theme's logo is made for its dark ground and does not), a folder theme's `logo.svg`, `.png`, `.webp` or `.jpg` beside its theme.css likewise;
- the **template family** (`family`): `calm`, the look of the stylesheet, for every theme unless set; or `playful`, the school family of `docs/slide-design-playful.md`, whose decoration (`src/domain/families.ts`) the generated stylesheet lays over the same layouts under the theme's selector: one disc per slide cut by the slide's edge, numerals in filled discs, the wavy line under titles, the facts of an exercise on the dark disc, figures whole on a disc. The accent leads, the second accent answers, the field colour is the dark pole; the family sets the kicker to the accent, the numerals to 800 and the marks to circles. A theme's family changes nothing for other themes.
- **own rules and files**, for a brand the tokens do not cover: CSS laid over everything the tokens and the family generate (`css`, up to 256 kB, no `@import`, no `</style>`), where `:scope` stands for the theme's selector so a copy keeps working, and the fonts and pictures it addresses relative to the stylesheet (`url(fonts/Brand.ttf)`), uploaded by `POST /api/themes/<id>/files` (file and path, up to 5 MB, a font or picture extension, served by it; the editor puts fonts under `fonts/`, pictures under `assets/`), removed by `DELETE …/files?path=…`, stored in `data/files/theme-files/<id>`; the generated stylesheet points the `url(…)` of every known file at its versioned address. A copy takes rules and files along, deleting the theme removes them. A whole folder theme moves into data this way: a partner's theme keeps the stylesheet of before the design pass of 2026-11 as its own rules and renders byte for byte as the folder did.
- a **pool** of pictures and icons (table `theme_assets`, assets of the assets table with a kind and tags): the generated title, agenda and break slides take the pictures in turn, in a fixed order per workshop (seeded by the workshop id, so a deck shows the same pictures every time); icons are offered to cards in the editor. A picture chosen by hand for one of these slides (in the editor, on the generated slide: from the pool, the workshop's pictures or an upload) is kept on the workshop (`pictures`: slide key → asset hash), shown to everyone, copied with a duplicate and spared by the sweep; "automatic" hands the slide back to the pool's turn. The pool is filled on the pictures page (an entry of the navigation: the default theme's pool, switchable between the data themes; files dropped onto the list or picked, several at once, tags typed as one line), in the theme editor, or by `POST /api/themes/<id>/pool` with a file or the hash of a known asset; `GET …/pool` lists it for everyone who sees the theme; a copy of the theme takes the pool along; the sweep leaves pool assets alone, and a workshop's guests may load them through the workshop's route.

The server generates the stylesheet from the tokens (`GET /api/themes/<id>/theme.css`, literal colours, no `color-mix`, so it prints and exports the same everywhere); the browser generates the same stylesheet for the draft while a theme is edited, so every change shows on sample slides at once. The editor checks the colour pairs that carry text against WCAG 2.2 (SC 1.4.3: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text from 24 px) and marks what falls short. Deleting a theme resets the workshops and the setting that used it to the default. Theme folders in `data/themes/<name>/theme.css` are listed alongside; a data theme with the same id hides a folder: `POST /api/themes` with an `id` takes a folder's name over, so workshops, decks and settings that name it keep it.

Fonts are self-hosted by the app: the standalone file inlines the Latin slices of the hosted fonts a theme names, so the deck shows the same type on a machine without them.

## 5. In the app (stage 3)

- **Slides are item content.** `Item.slides` is a list of `{ id, html }` next to the materials, so the library, inserting a copy, duplicating an item or a workshop and the connector carry slides along. Markers and notes have none. A workshop chooses its theme (`workshop.theme`, in its details), else the settings' default, else the built-in one.
- **Sanitised on write and on render.** The server stores a slide only after `src/domain/slides.ts` reduced it to the format and to exactly one `section.slide` root (anything after the root is dropped); the browser does the same again before it sets the markup, and the frame that shows a slide clips everything in it, `position: fixed` included (`contain: paint`). Pictures are `asset:<hash>` references; the host rewrites them to its API.
- **Assets** are raster pictures (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF; the bytes decide, never the file name), stored once by SHA-256 in `data/files/assets`, listed in the `assets` table, served inline and immutable. A workshop's guests load an asset through the workshop's route only while one of its slides shows it; who may edit the workshop (the owner too) also loads what it holds or has held, so a picture just uploaded shows in the editor before the slide is stored (these are the pictures an editor may place anyway); and, with the library switched on for them, through the library route only while a library entry they may read shows it (without the notes: not on a note item); the owner loads every library asset. `POST /api/assets/sweep` removes what no slide, library entry or pool deck shows any more and no theme's pool holds.
- **Cut-outs** (`server/cutout.ts`, docs/slide-design.md): a PNG uploaded with a flat background – the border pixels agree on one colour, the paper – is freed of it at upload: the ink is unmixed from the paper (every pixel as opaque as it is far from the paper colour, its colour the ink that over the paper gives it back), so the paper goes inside enclosed shapes too and the picture looks on any light ground as it would printed there, the effect of a multiply blend kept in the file; the result is trimmed to its subject and stored as an asset of its own that names its original (`cutout_of`). On a dark ground the ink would vanish and the paper's grain, kept as a faint opacity, would show as a grey veil: under a theme whose ground is dark (`dark` in the list of themes) the host asks for every picture with `?ground=dark`, and a cut-out then comes as its dark variant – the ink's lightness turned over, hue kept, the grain cleared, chalk on a board – made once from the cut-out and kept in `data/files/assets-dark`; a picture drawn in light ink stays as it is, since turned over it would vanish; photographs and originals come as they are, and the exports follow the host. The address is cached as immutable: a change to the routine needs that folder emptied and takes effect in a browser only once its cache has let go of the picture. The upload answers with both hashes, the editor shows the cut-out and lets a picture field switch back to the original (`GET …/assets/<hash>/info` names the pair); photographs and pictures that are transparent already are left alone; the sweep keeps an original and its cut-out together while one of them is used. Where an illustration stands whole on the ground – a card picture (`.plain` cards get `.contain`), the picture of a split (gets `.contain`), a main figure that does not fill the slide – the cut-out takes the original's place (`src/domain/cutouts.ts`): on a deck import, and for the slides already stored through `POST /api/assets/cutouts` (owner; workshops, library, pool, templates; the pictures on them get their cut-outs made with the current routine; running it again changes nothing).
- **The slide pool** (`decks`, `deck_slides`): translated decks uploaded once, owner only. The pool page (Slides → Pool in the navigation, `/slides/pool`) takes the preview folder of a translation: every `deck.json` found becomes a deck, its pictures are uploaded once, every slide checked, and on request the library entries the manifest proposes are created (existing titles skipped). Slides are copied from the pool into items and library entries; later changes to the pool leave the copies alone.
- **Item panel and library editor**: a strip of thumbnails; a click opens the slide editor (section 7) on the step or entry. On the library page, whoever only reads the library pages through an entry's slides without editing them. New slides come from a template, as a blank layout, from a picture or from existing slides (the pool and the library, section 7), and open in the editor at once. Agenda cards show the count next to the paperclip, and a click on it opens the editor; library cards show the first thumbnails, which open the editor too.
- **The deck tab** shows the whole deck in agenda order (`src/domain/deck.ts`): a title slide (one per day when there are several), an agenda slide per day with the computed times, the items' slides, and a break slide with its end time for every break without slides of its own; the agenda and break slides can be switched off. Divider slides for groups can be switched on (off unless asked for): a section slide with the group's title before the first step of every group that has a title and steps; they count in the section tracker like written section slides. Presenting is the main button of the workshop's header (the presenter view in its arrow); printing and exporting are in the header's Output menu (section 5a); the theme is chosen in the workshop's details, next to "Manage themes". A click on any slide opens the editor on the whole deck.
- **Presenting**: `/w/:id/present`, full screen, arrow keys, space and clicks, `f` for full screen, Escape leaves. **The presenter view** `/w/:id/presenter` is the facilitator's screen while the workshop runs: the current slide large, the next one small, the step's planned time against the clock (minutes left, or over), its facilitation notes. **All slides** (the grid button in its header, or G; as PowerPoint's "See all slides") shows the whole deck as thumbnails in place of the current and next slide, the slide shown marked and in the middle of the view; the arrow keys move through the grid (Home and End to the first and last), a click (or Enter) moves the presentation there and the view returns to its top, Escape or G goes back to where the view stood. The grid scrolls only its own box, never the page. Its Edit button opens the slide editor on the deck at the slide shown, and from there any slide; the audience stays on its slide while the editor pages, "Present from here" moves it. **Paging together**: every window of a workshop's presentation – the presentation, presenter views, a phone as remote – pages it and follows it. The server keeps the slide (`POST /api/workshops/:id/present` with the slide's deck key, its number and the window's tag; the live stream passes it on and tells a window that connects where the presentation stands), so a window opened later joins there; `?from=N` ("Present from here") leads from slide N instead. A slide is followed by its key, so slides added before it do not move the audience. Without the server the windows of one browser page together over a broadcast channel. Owner and edit guests lead; view guests are not told. Both views show the workshop live, so a change shows at once. On a phone the presenter view has large buttons at the thumb and pages with a swipe; both views keep the screen on. **Breaks set live**: a chip in the presenter view's header names the break shown or the next one; a tap opens its length and end, set one and the other follows (a length set beforehand counts from when the break's slide goes on screen). The server keeps these live times beside the slide shown, for every window that leads (`POST /api/workshops/:id/live`, src/domain/live.ts); they are not part of the plan. The presenter view shows the steps after the break at their new times with the plan beside them, and the generated break slide counts down ("noch 12 Minuten · bis 11:30", at the end "Es geht weiter"); the agenda slides, printouts and exports keep the plan. This is the simple live mode; more of it (concept.md, backlog) is still open. **Printing**: `/w/:id/print/slides`, 16:9 pages, one slide each. **The standalone file** carries the stylesheet, the theme with its fonts and logos inlined, the slices of Noto Sans JP its glyphs need, the pictures as data, and a few lines of script; it opens without a server and prints the same pages.
- **Themes**: the owner's themes as data (section 4), edited under Slides → Themes; theme folders `data/themes/<name>/theme.css` with fonts and logos beside them still work, served under `/api/themes`. The repository ships only the built-in default.
- **Template slides** (`slide_templates`, Slides → Templates): named slides to start from, shared by all workshops. A template is made from a layout or saved from the editor ("Save as template"), edited in the same editor, renamed, duplicated, ordered and deleted. Inserting one makes a copy; changing the template leaves the copies alone, like the library.
- **The connector** reads and writes slides as text, on steps and on library entries: `slide_format` hands the agent the rules and the catalogue with an example per layout (`src/domain/slideFormat.ts`), `slide_list`, `slide_get`, `slide_add`, `slide_update` (new markup, text replacements, a new position), `slide_remove` and `slide_set` change them, `pool_search`, `theme_pool` and `deck_outline` show the slide pool, the theme's picture pool and the deck; items take slides when they are created, and the theme is chosen per workshop from the list `settings_get` gives (built in, folders, data themes; docs/mcp.md). Themes and templates are not written through the connector yet.
- **Glyphs**: Noto Sans JP (sliced by unicode range, a dependency) sits behind every font stack of the format, so the kanji of section slides render on any machine; a slice is loaded only when a slide needs it.
- **Writing slides with an agent** is a project skill, `.claude/skills/slide-build/`: it carries the catalogue with an example, a rendering and what fits for every layout, the stylesheet and the default theme as copies, how the host renders, and the workflow from outline to upload. `scripts/slides/check.py` checks fragments against the format, runs the sanitiser, counts what is on the slide against the layout's capacity and renders the slides to find text that overflows; `scripts/slides/refresh-skill.py` renews the skill's copies, and the tests check that they are current. The installation's themes stay private and are added to a preview by hand.

## 5a. Export

The header's Output menu exports the deck as it is presented (the generated slides with the switches of the deck tab, the theme of the workshop, the footer line of themes that show one):

- **PowerPoint** (`src/slides/pptx/`, no dependency). Every slide is laid out in the page at its design size with the real stylesheet and theme, then read back: a stretch of text becomes a text box with its runs (font, size, weight as bold or not, italic, capitals, letter spacing, colour), placed where the browser set it, a little wider so a substituted font does not wrap at once, short left-set lines unwrapped; backgrounds and borders become rectangles, rounded rectangles or circles, borders on one side thin bars; the hard-edged radial gradients of the playful family become discs, linear gradients PowerPoint gradients; pictures and background pictures (the logo, the wave) are pictures, cropped as the slide crops them; an inline SVG is a picture in SVG with a PNG beside it for older readers, which PowerPoint can convert to shapes. Pseudo-elements (marks, numerals, rules, discs) are made real elements with the pseudo's computed style before measuring, with every element's computed style frozen inline so the inserted elements change no selector's match. The first `h1` or `h2` is the slide's title placeholder, so the outline, the slide list and screen readers have it. The file's theme takes the deck theme's colours and fonts, so a shape added in PowerPoint matches. `ooxml.ts` writes the package (presentation, one master and layout, theme, slides, media), `zip.ts` the archive (CRC-32, deflate through the platform's `CompressionStream`).
  - Limits: fonts are not embedded – a computer without the theme's fonts (Inter, Manrope, Lato, Source Serif 4 are free to install) shows a substitute, and lines may break differently; weights become bold or regular (the thin numerals are regular); a list is a text box per point with its mark as a shape, not a PowerPoint list; anything a theme's own CSS draws beyond backgrounds, borders, the two gradient kinds and pictures (shadows, masks, blend modes) is left out; no speaker notes.
  - Checked against LibreOffice's rendering of the file on all layouts in the default theme and in a playful data theme; not yet opened in PowerPoint itself.
- **PDF** (`src/slides/pdf/`, no dependency): a file written by the app, no print dialog. Every slide of the standalone deck (the HTML export's pieces: stylesheet, theme, inlined fonts and pictures) is put into an SVG `foreignObject`, drawn by the browser onto a canvas at twice the design size (2560 × 1440, about 190 dpi on the 16:9 page) and stored as a JPEG page; the slide's words, read from the same layout, lie over it as invisible Helvetica text scaled to each word's box, so the file can be searched and text selected and copied. The look is exactly the browser's; text is not vector, so it softens at strong zoom, and a deck of 25 slides is about 2.5 MB. A vector PDF would have to embed the fonts – the hosted ones are variable fonts, which PDF cannot use – and was not worth a font library. The print view (`/w/:id/print/slides`) remains for paper, with the backgrounds printed without the browser's background option.
- **HTML**: the standalone file of section 5.
- **The theme in the files.** HTML and PDF fetch the theme's stylesheet once more to inline its fonts and logos. If that fails (network, server, an expired login), the export stops with a message instead of writing slides without their theme; a theme deleted meanwhile (404) gives the built-in theme, and the slides then name it, so its rules apply. PowerPoint reads the slides from the page itself and needs no second fetch.

## 6. Translating existing decks (stage 2)

The workflow is a project skill, `.claude/skills/deck-translate/SKILL.md`, with the tools in `scripts/slides/`:

1. **Extract**: `extract.py deck.pptx OUTDIR` reads the PPTX with the standard library only and writes `deck.json` (every slide: layout, background, shapes in z-order with position, fill and text, pictures, tables, charts, SmartArt texts, speaker notes), `outline.md` and `media/` (pictures by content hash).
2. **Cut**: the deck's `deck.json` in the preview folder lists the library entries the deck becomes: title, kind, category, duration, method, material, notes (the tidied speaker notes) and the slides of each. Title, agenda, break and closing slides belong to no entry, since the app generates them; one-off client material is listed as skipped.
3. **Rewrite**: Claude writes one fragment per slide into the catalogue. Only the deck's words; a line a layout needs but the deck lacks is marked `class="todo"` and shows up in the preview. Pictures become assets, simple shapes become SVG, decorative template art is dropped.
4. **Review**: `build.py ROOT` assembles the preview (grid in deck order with the entry headings, click to present, `?only=N` and `?sheet=K` for screenshots), `render.sh ROOT/deck.html` writes a screenshot per slide and contact sheets of six with headless Chromium. Every sheet is looked at before hand-over.
5. **Import**: Slide pool → Upload deck, with the preview folder. The decks found become pool decks; the manifest's entries become library entries on request.

**Without an AI**: the slide pool also takes a `.pptx` directly and reads it in the browser by rules (`src/slides/pptx/read.ts`). The aim is the content in a usable shape, not the file's layouts: every word once, under a title, in reading order, in the plainest layout that holds it. A slide the next one repeats with more on it (a build made by copying) or exactly is left out; text boxes that stand in columns (each under a heading of its own, or closer under each other than the columns are beside each other) are read column by column, a column under its heading; a picture and the text right beside it are one part, shown as a card with its picture (two rows that do not fit become one row), or, where the text is too long for a card, on a slide of its own with the picture whole, after a slide that shows the parts together by name; pictures that pair with no text follow on a slide of their own, and a tiny picture counts only where it sits on a text, as the icon of a heading; a lone text box gives its first set-apart line as the title; a table set from right to left is turned round; a hyphen typed to break a word goes where the deck writes the word in one piece elsewhere. What cannot come across is named per slide. `SP_DECKS=folder npm run import:report` prints what the import makes of the decks in a folder (slides made per slide of the file, repeated titles, slides without a title, words that went missing), for judging a change to the rules against real decks; a rule goes in only when it helps unrelated decks and harms none.

The first deck translated this way, a four-day leadership seminar of 61 slides, became 46 library entries without a todo; the regular structure of its slides (inputs as cards, exercises with the facts panel, questions as statements) maps onto the catalogue one to one. Decks built on older templates will need more judgement per slide, in particular grouped shapes and SmartArt.

## 7. Editing (stage 4)

The editor works on content, the layout arranges it, the theme colours it. Three layers, each edited in its own place, none of them in pixels. What the research into slide tools agreed on went into it:

- **Constrained layouts beat free placement.** Beautiful.ai's Smart Slides are templates that act as constraints: when content is added, the layout adapts, text resizes, spacing adjusts, and a slide that no longer fits is fixed by choosing another template, not by nudging boxes (Beautiful.ai blog, "What the heck are Smart Slides"; third-party reviews 2026). Gamma edits cards whose layout type, column count and blocks can be changed per card while the theme stays the same everywhere (Gamma guides, "How Gamma maps content directly to slides using its card system"). Marp states "separation of content and style" as its design principle: content in Markdown, appearance in a theme stylesheet (marp.app/docs). The catalogue of section 3 is this app's set of constraints; the editor only ever fills it.
- **Three layers, as in PowerPoint and Google Slides.** A theme holds colours and fonts, a master's layouts hold the arrangement and the placeholders, a slide holds content; global formatting belongs on the master or layout, never on the slide, one master per deck, layouts with clear names (think-cell, "How to use and edit slide masters"; pptproductivity, "Difference between templates, themes and layouts"; Google Workspace Updates 2021, Theme builder). Here: theme (section 4), the fixed catalogue with its stylesheet (section 3), template slides as named starting points, the slide.
- **A brand kit keeps a few colours, the fonts and the logo in one place** and applies them to every design (Canva Brand Kit). The theme editor shows ten colours and derives the rest, so a brand is set in minutes and stays consistent.
- **Readable text is a check, not a taste.** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold); the ratio is (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05) over the relative luminances. The theme editor computes it for every pair that carries text.
- **On a phone, controls live at the bottom and in one sheet at a time**; nothing hides behind hover (bottom-sheet guidance in design systems and the LogRocket article on bottom sheets). The editor on a phone is one screen: the slide on top, the fields below, previous and next at the thumb.
- **contentEditable only where it is safe** (2026-11-17, revising "contentEditable is avoided" of 2026-11-01). Its known problems – cursor jumps when the page re-renders, pasted formatting, output that needs cleaning, uneven behaviour on phones (react-sane-contenteditable's issue list; editor vendors' "paste as plain text" modes) – are met one by one where a mouse is used, and avoided altogether where it is not (see "On the slide" below). The fields stay plain inputs and stay the way to edit on a phone and a touch screen. Inline emphasis is written in them as `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `==accent==`, as in the notes; a marker counts only at a word's edge (Markdown's rule for the underscore), so gender stars („Teilnehmer*innen“), `a==b` and `2 * 3` stay text; a marker that would count is written `\*` to keep it literal. Formatting by hand (section 2) is a span in brackets with what it sets in braces: `[text]{l accent-2}`, with the words `xs s l xl`, `accent accent-2 accent-3 accent-4 title text muted`, `normal`, and `b` and `i` for bold and italic inside a word, where the stars do not count (`Kolleg[innen]{i}`); a literal bracket that would be read as one is written `\[`.

[BELEG?] The reviews of Beautiful.ai and the Gamma guides are vendor or third-party pages read in November 2026; they describe the products, not measured outcomes.

**The slide document** (`src/domain/slideDoc.ts`). A slide's markup is read into a document: its layout, the layout's options (the modifier classes of section 3 as flags and a column count), a header (eyebrow, title, lead, the tag of an exercise), the content part the layout holds – points, cards, steps and facts, a quote, a figure, text blocks, agenda rows, the four cells of a matrix, letters, boxes, a table, the SVG of a model – and the source line. Every field is written back in the catalogue's markup, so the sanitiser leaves it unchanged and the stylesheet knows it. Every slide is checked when it is opened: only when writing the document back shows the same slide (whitespace, attribute order and default classes aside) are its fields offered. Otherwise the body is edited as source with header and footer as fields, and when even those cannot be held (an extra paragraph in the header, emphasis inside a word, `sub`/`sup`), the whole slide is source. Opening a slide and changing one field therefore never changes anything else; a slide kept as source keeps its layout. `LAYOUTS` is the catalogue as a schema: which parts a layout shows, which options it has, how many entries it holds before the slide is over-full.

**Changing the layout** carries the content: the title stays the title, points become cards become steps (headings and texts kept where the new layout has both), a picture stays where the layout shows one, the source stays in the footer. The chooser shows every layout with this slide's content in it, as it would stand there (the six shapes of a diagram as entries of their own, in a group "Diagrams"; picking another shape for a slide that is a diagram already keeps its entries and its middle word), and names under the thumbnail what the layout could not hold – "2 entries do not fit", "the picture has no place" – before it is chosen; after the change the editor says it again, with the way back next to it. A slide kept as source is not converted, so there is nothing to show for it.

**Looking first.** Most of the time a slide is opened to look at it, not to change it. So the editor opens as a viewer: the slide as large as the window allows, the thumbnails below it, the header with where it comes from and the position; the arrows, a swipe on a phone or a thumbnail page. "Edit" (or E, or a double-click on a text of the slide, which puts the caret into that text – on a touch screen into its field, at the end) brings the fields; "Done" or Escape goes back to looking, with what was typed stored. Paging keeps the mode. A slide just added (from a template or a layout, "Edit slides" under a step's thumbnails, the pencil of a template) opens straight into editing. Slides that are not edited here – generated, from the pool, a guest's view – stay in the viewer with their note and what can be done instead. Undo and redo work while looking too. From the planner "Present from here" (⋯ menu) opens the presentation at that slide, as long as the slide is in the deck (a parked step's slides are not).

**The editor** (`src/slides/editor`). In editing, on a desktop the slide is large on the left with the thumbnails of the step's slides below, the fields on the right: the layout's options first, then header, content, footer. Entries (points, cards, steps …) are added, moved and removed in place; the count against the layout's room is shown; a picture is chosen from the ones the workshop already shows or uploaded. The slide reports when its content runs over the edge, so the choice is to shorten or to pick a roomier layout. Edits are stored a moment after typing stops, always on the slide they were typed into. The source is still there, behind the ⋯ menu, for what the fields do not reach; what is typed there is applied when the field is left and before the editor pages or closes.

**On the slide** (`EditPreview.tsx`, `inplace.ts`, `SlideToolbar.tsx`; `src/domain/slideEdit.ts`, `inlineRuns.ts`). With a mouse or trackpad on more than a phone's width (`pointer: fine`), editing happens on the slide itself; the fields beside it stay and follow.

- **A click on a text puts the caret into it**, where the click was. Nothing on a slide can be dragged, so a click need not select first, as it must in tools where a click may begin a drag (Keynote, Google Slides, Figma: click selects, double-click edits; Notion and Gutenberg, which like this editor only flow, put the caret on one click and reach the block with Escape). Escape or Enter leaves the text selected, Escape again selects nothing, once more leaves editing; Shift+Enter is a line break; Tab goes to the next text or picture, Shift+Tab back. With an element selected, Enter types into it and Delete removes it – never while the caret is in a text.
- **A bar above the selection**: bold, italic, smaller, larger, a colour of the palette, "remove formatting" (only where something is formatted by hand), and remove. It acts on the selected words, or on the whole text when none are selected or the text is only selected, not typed in. Bold on a heading the theme sets bold takes it off (`fw-normal`). The bar stands in the window's own size, outside the scaled slide, and never takes the focus, so the selection it acts on stays.
- **A click on a picture selects it**: another one from the picker (the theme's pool, the pictures used nearby, an upload), its cut-out or original, remove; a double-click opens the picker, and a picture file dropped onto a picture takes its place. A click beside the words of a card, a box or a row selects the entry, and the bar of a text or picture inside one has "select the whole entry", to remove it.
- **What can go, goes; what stands in a fixed place is emptied**: a cell of a matrix or a table would move the others. A removal that would leave a slide the layout's fields cannot hold (one side of a comparison) empties the element instead, and where even that does not fit, it is refused with a pointer to the fields. Removing does not ask; the notice offers undo.
- **How it stays safe.** One text element at a time is editable. What is typed is read from the element after every keystroke as runs – stretches of text with their formatting, line breaks, and islands for what a text holds but the editor does not model (the tag of an exercise title, a nested list) – and written into the slide's markup at the element's place, then read as a slide document like any other change, so the fields, the stored slide and undo see the same thing. A change after which the layout's fields could no longer hold the slide is not taken. A formatting command changes the runs and builds the element's content from them, always nested the same way (span, strong, em), with the selection kept as positions in the runs. While a text is typed in, the markup React renders is held still, so the caret never jumps; when the text is left, the slide is rendered afresh from what was stored. Paste puts in plain text on one line; the browser's own formatting, undo and drops are switched off in favour of the editor's. Typing in one text is one step of the history while it goes on; Ctrl+Z takes it back and leaves the caret in the text.
- **Not on the slide**: generated slides (their words come from the workshop), the labels inside an SVG diagram, and adding what is not there yet (a new card, a picture where none is): the fields do that. A slide whose markup the browser had to repair (a table without its tbody) opens the field instead.
- **Phones and touch screens** keep the fields only (2026-11-17). A slide on a phone is about 360 px wide, its text 7 px high; the keyboard covers half the screen, the system's selection menu cannot be switched off and lies over any bar at the selection (ProseMirror forum, "How to disable the mobile Safari selection menu"), and whether the caret sits right inside a scaled element on touch browsers has no current, authoritative answer (WebKit bugs 19058, 259964, 300690 are of this kind), so it would have to be tried on devices first. Formatting is written there in the fields' markup.

[BELEG?] How the other tools behave is from their help pages read in November 2026 (Keynote for iPhone, Google Slides shortcuts, Notion shortcuts, Gutenberg's navigation and edit mode); Canva, Gamma and PowerPoint for the web could not be read first-hand.

**New slides** start from a starter, a template (the owner's), a blank layout (every layout of the catalogue as a thumbnail in the current theme), a picture, or existing slides: the pool's decks and the library entries that hold slides are the sources of one picker (one source at a time, a search goes over all of them, several slides are taken at once as copies); the first three open the editor at once. In the editor they go after the slide shown, in its step.

**Starter slides** (`src/domain/starters.ts`, `sampleSets.ts`) are the first page of "New slide", for everyone who may edit: every layout the way it is meant to be used, in the variants the real decks use most (points with a heading, numbered cards, an exercise with its facts, the six diagram shapes …), grouped by what a slide does – one thing large, side by side, one after another, how it hangs together, with a picture. They are filled with a sample subject, made up and a little silly so nobody takes it for content (an office outing to the moon, the perfect office coffee, keeping the office plant alive, the team picnic, a treasure hunt through the office building), in German or English after the app's language. Every starter draws its own subject and its own variant of it at random when the picker opens – which of a subject's six points it shows, in which order – so the page is mixed and no two openings look alike; "Shuffle" draws again, and one subject can be chosen for all starters instead. The draw is seeded, so the page stands still while it is looked at. Starters with pictures take them from the theme's pool; a layout made for a picture that has none shows a dashed field where it would stand, in the pickers and while editing only. Starters are code, not data: they follow the catalogue and need no upkeep by the owner, whose own templates stay beside them.

**Sample text is there to be replaced.** A text that is still a sample's (or a blank layout's placeholder word) is selected as a whole when it is clicked on the slide or its field gets the focus, so the first key typed replaces it. What is left is found by comparison with the samples, word for word, with no mark in the slide: the editor names how many sample phrases a slide still shows, and the workshop's Slides tab names the slides that do, above the deck, before it is presented or exported. Only phrases count there; a single word of a sample ("Risks") may be a real slide's own.

**Sorting, moving and removing** (`src/slides/SlideSort.tsx`, `src/domain/slideMove.ts`). Whoever may edit does this on the thumbnails, without opening a slide: in a step's details, in the workshop's Slides tab and on a library entry.

- **Dragging** a thumbnail sorts: dropped on the left half of another it goes before it, on the right half after it, and a line shows where; nothing moves before the drop. In the Slides tab the slide joins the step of the slide it is dropped on, so dragging across the edge of a step moves it there; the generated slides stay where the plan puts them and are no targets. Near the edge of a long list it scrolls. Dragging is for the mouse; on touch screens the menu does the same.
- **The menu** of a thumbnail (⋯, shown when the thumbnail is pointed at or has the focus, always on touch screens): "Move to …" (in a workshop) lists the steps that can carry slides by day, the parking lot last, with the number of slides each has; the step the slide is in is marked, a full one (60 slides) cannot be chosen, and the slide goes to the end of the step chosen. "Duplicate" puts a copy after the slide, "Remove" takes it away.
- In a workshop every one of these is one change of the planner and so one undo; removing does not ask, and a notice names the undo. On a library entry, which has no undo, removing asks first.
- A slide keeps its id when it moves (a new one only if the target already holds a slide of that id). Its key in the deck names its step too: a presentation that shows a slide while it moves to another step stays at that slide's number.

The editor's ⋯ menu has "Move to …" as well, next to moving a slide left and right within its step: the editor follows the slide where it still shows it (on the deck), else it goes on with the next slide, and closes when the step it is open on has none left.

**One way to open a slide, everywhere** (`src/slides/editor/source.ts`). A slide thumbnail outside a picker opens that slide in the editor; in a picker (new slide, pool) a thumbnail selects. The editor pages within what it was opened from, and its header says what that is:

| Opened from | Pages through | Can be changed |
|---|---|---|
| a step's details, the slide count on its agenda card | the step's slides | everything |
| the deck tab | the whole deck in agenda order, the steps' names between the thumbnails | the steps' slides; generated slides are shown with where their content comes from |
| a library card or entry | the entry's slides | everything |
| the templates | all templates in their order | everything; inserting makes a template |
| the pool | the deck of the pool | nothing (the pool is a source); "Save as template" |

Generated slides in the deck say what they are made of and what can be done instead: the title slide opens the workshop details; agenda and break slides can be hidden; a break can get a slide of its own (a copy of the generated one, from then on its slide). The editor works on a source of the same shape in every case – the slides with their keys and groups, and writing, inserting, removing and moving – so paging, editing and undo behave the same everywhere.

**History.** The editor keeps its own history (`src/slides/editor/history.ts`): Ctrl+Z (⌘Z), Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y, and buttons in its header. Every change is a step – typing in a field (one step while it goes on; a pause of 1.5 s or leaving the field ends it), a layout or option, a picture, inserting, duplicating, removing and moving a slide, within its step or into another. Undo works inside the fields too, so it means the same everywhere in the editor; only the source view keeps the field's own undo until it is applied. A step changed elsewhere since (another window, an agent) is not taken back – it is dropped with a note, rather than overwriting the other change. In the planner the whole session is one undo step: after closing the editor, one Ctrl+Z takes back everything done in it.

**Focus and keys.** The editor, like every dialog, sheet and menu of the app (`src/components/overlay.ts`), takes the focus when it opens, keeps Tab inside, and gives the focus back to what opened it when it closes. Escape leaves a field or a text on the slide, then the selection on the slide, then editing, then closes; a menu or picker above it takes Escape first. The arrows page while no field has the focus. The planner's shortcuts wait while anything lies above it.

**The slides area.** What slides are made from – the pool, the templates, the themes – is one entry of the navigation, Slide pool (Folienpool), with a tab each (`/slides/pool`, `/slides/templates`, `/slides/themes`; the old addresses lead there). Wherever a theme is chosen (a workshop's details, the settings) "Manage themes" sits next to it; wherever templates are offered (new slide) "Manage templates" does. The area is the owner's: a guest who types its address lands on the workshops shared with them.

## 8. Open points

- Whether the facts panel of an exercise is always generated from the item, or may be written in the slide.
- Charts: inline SVG drawn by the translation for now; a chart element of its own only if it is needed often.
- The partner theme's speech bubble is drawn after the slide master as an SVG background; the fine shape of its curve may still change.
- Accepted (2026-11-02): two people changing different slides of the same step within the same moment: the later save wins (the slides of a step are one field). The presentation's slide lives in the server's memory: after a restart the windows start where their links say.
- Editing on the slide on tablets (tap to select, a second tap to type, the bar fixed at the slide's edge), once tried on devices; labels inside SVG diagrams; Enter in a point for a new point; literal colours, should the palette ever be too narrow (a narrow exception in the sanitiser, and they would not follow a dark theme).
- Themes and templates through the connector (an agent setting up a client's colours from a style guide); a dark variant derived from a light theme with one click; moving a slide between items through the connector, and several slides at once.
